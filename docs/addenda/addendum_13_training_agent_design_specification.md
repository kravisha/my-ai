## Agent Training, Testing, Evaluation, Reinforcement, and Knowledge Preservation

### Status

This document consolidates the training-agent design decisions from the current architecture discussion. It is intended as an implementation-facing addendum to the existing system specifications.

### Prime objective

Training exists to improve the organization's ability to identify meaningful market opportunities, reason about them correctly, and ultimately improve the quality and calibration of forward-looking market predictions. Training must reward signal quality, not activity volume.

### 1. Core Training Principle

**Agents are replaceable; organizational knowledge is indispensable.**

The Training Agent does not merely teach an individual process. Its deeper responsibility is to develop, validate, reinforce, and preserve organizational capability so that lessons survive the lifecycle of any particular agent.

- Training must be evidence-driven.
- Every exercise produces measurable results.
- Every failure should produce a reusable lesson when a valid lesson can be established.
- Every important production miss should be considered as a candidate for future training and regression coverage.
- Training quality is measured by downstream agent performance, not by the number of exercises produced.

### 2. One Training Agent, Multiple Logical Functions

The initial design does **not** require separate permanent Test Development, Test Execution, and Training agents. One Training Agent may perform the complete loop, but the responsibilities must remain logically distinct so they can be separated later if scale, independence, or complexity requires it.

- **Test and scenario design:** define competencies, build test suites, create simulated conditions, establish hidden expected outcomes, and define scoring criteria.
- **Exercise orchestration:** provide the target agent with simulated inputs or tasks and allow the target agent to operate normally.
- **Assessment:** compare produced results with expected results and evaluate accuracy, reasoning, completeness, relevance, confidence, efficiency, and role-specific behavior.
- **Reinforcement:** provide positive reinforcement for demonstrated capability and corrective/negative feedback for failures, including an explanation of what should change.
- **Knowledge capture:** convert validated lessons into durable organizational knowledge and future training material.

### 3. Competency-Based Test Design

**Start from competencies, not from arbitrary tests.**

For every agent role, first define what that agent must be capable of doing. Every important competency must then map to measurable, repeatable tests. Every test must trace back to a required competency.

- Tests should cover normal cases, obvious failures, subtle cases, boundary conditions, noise, missing data, contradictory evidence, false positives, and adversarial conditions.
- Tests should become progressively more difficult as capability improves.
- A single pass is not sufficient evidence of competence. Repeated and varied scenarios should establish that success is reproducible rather than accidental.
- The curriculum must evolve as markets, system behavior, and discovered weaknesses evolve.

### 4. Explorer Training

Explorer training should optimize for discovering genuine anomalies and meaningful candidates while suppressing noise and weak signals.

- Detect known injected anomalies reliably.
- Distinguish normal variation from meaningful dislocation.
- Handle volatility surfaces, peer relationships, noise, missing data, threshold boundaries, isolated events, and peer-wide events.
- Measure false positives as well as missed opportunities.
- Reward useful discovery quality rather than report volume.
- Train the Explorer to investigate sufficiently before escalating, while preserving its role boundary as a discovery agent rather than a final investment analyst.

### 5. Speculator Training

Speculator training should optimize for finding meaningful contextual evidence and separating useful signal from rumor, noise, popularity, and weak narratives.

- Use simulated streams containing useful chatter, irrelevant chatter, coordinated noise, rumors, conflicting evidence, delayed corroboration, attention bursts, and false leads.
- Test source attribution, provenance, corroboration, confidence, novelty, and relevance.
- Penalize promotion of rumor to fact without adequate evidence.
- Reward evidence that materially improves the identification or interpretation of a potential opportunity.
- Train for strong signals, not simply more signals.

### 6. Analysis Agent Training

Analysis training should optimize for correct, evidence-based interpretation of candidate opportunities.

- Combine quantitative evidence, peer context, social/contextual evidence, uncertainty, and other permitted information.
- Test the ability to reject weak opportunities rather than manufacture a thesis.
- Evaluate factual correctness, completeness, reasoning quality, competing explanations, uncertainty handling, confidence calibration, usefulness, and reasons the thesis could be wrong.
- Later observed market outcomes should feed back into Analysis training and confidence calibration.
- The Analysis Agent should learn that 'not enough evidence' and 'not worthwhile' are valid conclusions.

### 7. Training Modes

The training architecture should support multiple sources of learning. The immediate design recognizes at least the following two core modes, while leaving room for additional modes later.

- **Simulated training:** trainer-generated scenarios with controlled conditions and hidden ground truth. This is the initial day-zero training environment.
- **Real-world / experience-based learning:** evaluated historical and production observations become training evidence. Real outcomes reveal whether prior reasoning, confidence, and opportunity selection were actually useful.

The two modes form a loop: simulation builds foundational capability; real-world evidence exposes weaknesses and new patterns; those discoveries are converted back into improved simulations and regression cases.

### 8. Reinforcement and Corrective Feedback

When an agent succeeds, the Training Agent records what capability was demonstrated and provides positive reinforcement. When an agent fails, the Training Agent must do more than assign a failing grade.

- Identify what was wrong or insufficient.
- Explain the expected behavior or reasoning at the appropriate level of detail.
- Capture the lesson so it is not lost with the current agent instance.
- Create or update follow-up exercises that verify the correction.
- Repeat until the required competency is demonstrated with sufficient consistency.

Feedback must not teach agents to memorize one test answer. The objective is transferable capability.

### 9. Organizational Knowledge Preservation

**Knowledge must be persisted as organizational data, not left only inside an agent's transient context.**

Validated lessons, test outcomes, corrections, training history, FAQs, decision rationale, useful patterns, and other durable intelligence should be stored in a shared organizational knowledge base. Agents may be suspended, restarted, retrained, or replaced without losing validated knowledge.

- The knowledge base is the organizational source of truth.
- Agents contribute to it and retrieve from it according to role and need.
- The Training Agent is a major producer and curator of validated training knowledge.
- Knowledge records should remain attributable and versioned so the organization can understand where a lesson came from and whether later evidence changed it.

### 10. Two-Layer Knowledge Model — Initial Version

The first implementation should deliberately keep knowledge classification simple.

- **Resident knowledge:** information stored in the database and also loaded or embedded with the agent because it is sufficiently important to be available during normal operation without an explicit retrieval step.
- **Database knowledge:** information stored durably in the organizational knowledge base but retrieved only when needed.

The database remains the canonical source of truth for both classes. Resident knowledge is a working copy or agent-available representation, not an independent competing source.

More sophisticated hot-cache/archive tiers may be introduced later only when actual operating experience demonstrates a need.

### 11. Persistence and Database Architecture

**Implementation task:** introduce a stable database/knowledge access abstraction so agents never depend directly on a specific database engine.

- The current implementation may continue using SQLite for the initial build.
- All organizational persistence and knowledge access should go through a defined data-access or knowledge-service interface.
- Database-specific assumptions must not leak throughout individual agents.
- This abstraction must make later migration to PostgreSQL possible with minimal changes to agent logic.
- When semantic retrieval becomes necessary, PostgreSQL with pgvector is a suitable future path because structured relational data and vector search can coexist behind the same persistence boundary.
- Caches may improve performance but must never become the sole durable copy of organizational knowledge.

### 12. Every Agent Can Be Trained

The training model is universal. Explorer, Speculator, Analysis, COO, HR, Controller, Bob/CEO, Training Agents, and future agents are all subject to training and evaluation appropriate to their responsibilities.

- Executive status does not exempt an agent from demonstrated competence.
- Training Agents themselves must be evaluated.
- HR manages the training function and training standards.
- Independent evaluation is required for functions that would otherwise become self-certifying.
- The same broad lifecycle applies: create -> train -> validate -> operate -> evaluate -> improve -> suspend/retire -> resume -> revalidate.

### 13. Training Agent Evaluation

A Training Agent must be judged by whether its training produces better agents and better organizational outcomes.

- Scenario realism.
- Coverage of required competencies.
- Ability to expose weaknesses and defects.
- False confidence avoided: tests must not merely make trainees pass.
- Quality and accuracy of expected results / ground truth.
- Usefulness of corrective feedback.
- Improvement demonstrated by trainees after feedback.
- Predictive usefulness of simulated certification for later historical or live performance.
- Quality of knowledge captured for reuse.

### 14. Continuous Learning Loop

**Simulation -> Exercise -> Evaluation -> Feedback -> Knowledge Capture -> Retraining -> Revalidation -> Production Outcome -> Curriculum Improvement**

This loop should operate continuously. Production is not separate from learning: evaluated real-world work supplies evidence about what the organization actually understands and where it remains weak.

- Every discovered bug or repeatable failure should become a candidate permanent regression scenario.
- Every validated new insight should be considered for incorporation into the knowledge base and future curriculum.
- Every production miss should trigger the question: was this a capability gap, a data gap, a reasoning gap, a confidence-calibration problem, or unavoidable uncertainty?
- Production behavior changes remain gated by validation. Continuous learning does not mean uncontrolled self-modification.

### 15. Implementation Tasks

- Define a common Training Agent interface and role contract.
- Define competency records and competency-to-test traceability.
- Define scenario/test records with version, seed, inputs, hidden ground truth, expected behavior, scoring rubric, and target role.
- Implement simulated scenario generation for Explorer and Speculator using the already specified scenario families.
- Define and implement Analysis training scenarios.
- Implement exercise orchestration and result collection.
- Implement multidimensional grading and reinforcement feedback.
- Persist every training run, result, grade, feedback artifact, agent/model version, and lesson learned.
- Implement the shared organizational knowledge record format.
- Implement the two-layer resident/database knowledge policy.
- Implement a database abstraction/knowledge-service layer over the current SQLite persistence.
- Ensure the persistence interface can later migrate to PostgreSQL and optionally pgvector without redesigning agent logic.
- Implement regression promotion: validated failures become reusable future tests.
- Implement trainer evaluation based on trainee and downstream performance.

### 16. Canonical Design Invariants

- Training is a first-class organizational capability.
- Tests derive from competencies.
- Every important competency has measurable and repeatable tests.
- One Training Agent may initially perform test design, orchestration, assessment, feedback, and knowledge capture, but those responsibilities remain logically separable.
- Training optimizes signal quality and predictive usefulness, not activity volume.
- Success and failure both produce organizational evidence.
- Validated knowledge survives the agent that learned it.
- The database is the canonical durable knowledge source.
- The initial knowledge model has only two classes: resident knowledge and database-only knowledge.
- SQLite may remain initially, but persistence must sit behind a swappable abstraction layer.
- All agents, including trainers and executives, are trainable and evaluable.
- Real-world outcomes feed back into simulation, curriculum, grading, and confidence calibration.
- Continuous learning is permitted; production changes remain validation-gated.

### 17. Relationship to Existing Specifications

This design extends the existing training specification rather than discarding it. The current specification already establishes testing as a first-class function, adversarial trainer behavior, hidden ground truth, simulation fidelity, reproducible scenarios, regression testing, maturity stages, continuous learning, and health/refresh behavior. This document adds the competency-first training methodology, the unified initial Training Agent responsibilities, reinforcement/correction loop, explicit organizational knowledge preservation, the two-layer knowledge model, and the SQLite-to-PostgreSQL-ready persistence abstraction.

Where implementation encounters a genuine conflict with an older requirement, it must stop at that decision point and reconcile the canonical specification rather than silently implementing contradictory alternatives.
