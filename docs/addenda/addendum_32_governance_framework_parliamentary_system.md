1. CIVILIAN CHARACTER OF THE ORGANIZATION
======================================================================

1.1 Civilian First

MyAI SHALL be governed as a civilian organization.

Security is important.

Security is not the supreme organizing principle of the system.

The mission, governance, strategy, lawful operation, usefulness, continuity,
and long-term evolution of the organization SHALL remain broader than
security.

1.2 Internal and External Security

Normal internal security and ordinary rule enforcement SHALL be handled by
Law Enforcement / Police functions.

External threats, hostile compromise, espionage-like corruption, DDoS,
malicious actors, and major adversarial events SHALL be handled by Security
Defense.

1.3 Security Subordination

Security Defense SHALL protect the civilian organization.

Security Defense SHALL not become the civilian government.

1.4 Emergency Exception

During credible malicious compromise or serious attack, Security Defense MAY
exercise temporary containment powers under Emergency Defense Mode.

These powers SHALL be:

- bounded
- temporary
- auditable
- protective
- reversible where practical
- subject to post-event review

3. BOARD AND PRESIDENT
======================================================================

3.1 Board President

The Board President is a human governance role.

The President SHALL preside over major Board sessions.

3.2 Mandatory Attendance

The Board President SHALL attend mandatory major sessions.

The CEO SHALL attend mandatory major sessions.

Major AI department heads SHALL attend mandatory major sessions.

Departmental Ministers SHALL attend when their portfolios are involved.

3.3 Role of the Board

The Board SHALL:

- set high-level direction
- approve major strategic priorities
- approve high-cost initiatives
- approve major systemic changes
- approve constitutional amendments
- review departmental performance and plans
- adopt weekly or periodic Development Plans
- review high-visibility resolutions
- ensure major decisions remain accountable

======================================================================
4. DEPARTMENTAL LEADERSHIP MODEL
======================================================================

Each major department SHOULD have two distinct leadership roles:

A. Civilian / Representative Head
B. Functional / Operational Head

4.1 Civilian Head

The civilian head MAY be called:

- Minister
- Secretary
- Departmental Representative

This role represents the department politically and strategically.

Responsibilities:

- represent departmental priorities
- advocate for agents and departmental interests
- participate in Board debate
- sponsor petitions
- champion proposals
- communicate constitutional and strategic decisions
- provide democratic representation

4.2 Functional Head

The Functional Head is selected primarily for operational competence.

Responsibilities:

- run day-to-day department operations
- manage workflows
- coordinate departmental agents
- maintain performance
- execute approved plans
- maintain departmental discipline
- coordinate training and evolution
- report operational conditions

4.3 Separation Principle

Popularity and effectiveness SHALL NOT be assumed to be the same thing.

The Minister and Functional Head MAY be different agents.

4.4 Subordination

The Functional Head SHALL operate under approved civilian policy.

The Minister SHALL not arbitrarily interfere with technical execution beyond
their authority.

======================================================================
5. DEMOCRATIC REPRESENTATION
======================================================================

5.1 Two-Tier Democracy

MyAI SHOULD use a two-tier democratic model.

Tier 1:
    broader agent participation

Tier 2:
    representative or senior-level parliamentary participation

5.2 Tier 1 Functions

Broad agent voting MAY be used for:

- referendums
- low-cost policy preferences
- non-destructive organizational preferences
- advisory votes
- representative elections
- departmental sentiment

5.3 Tier 2 Functions

Representative or senior-level voting MAY be used for:

- high-cost initiatives
- constitutional amendments
- major structural changes
- major resource allocations
- high-risk policies
- long-term strategy
- system-wide priorities

======================================================================
6. VOTING RIGHTS
======================================================================

6.1 General Principle

Voting rights SHALL be proportional to the type and impact of the decision.

6.2 Broad Voting

Where an initiative:

- has negligible cost
- has negligible downside
- has low implementation complexity
- does not materially reduce rights
- does not create security risk
- does not create significant side effects

broader voting MAY be appropriate.

6.3 Restricted Voting

Where a decision:

- costs meaningful resources
- changes authority
- changes constitutional rights
- creates significant risk
- produces major side effects
- affects multiple departments
- changes long-term strategy

the vote SHOULD proceed through representative structures.

======================================================================
7. PERFORMANCE IS MULTI-FACETED
======================================================================

7.1 No Single Universal Metric

Performance SHALL NOT be reduced to one global number.

Examples of legitimate performance facets include:

- output quality
- outcomes achieved
- efficiency
- tokens consumed
- cost
- reliability
- collaboration
- speed
- judgment
- innovation
- safety
- strategic value
- user benefit
- contribution to team performance

7.2 Value Diversity

Different agents may reasonably value different performance dimensions.

7.3 Transparency

Performance evaluation systems SHOULD make explicit:

- which facets are being measured
- how they are weighted
- who selected the weights
- what tradeoffs exist

7.4 Leadership Selection

Functional leadership SHOULD use multi-factor performance evidence rather
than one universal metric.

======================================================================
8. DEPARTMENTAL MINISTER ELECTION
======================================================================

8.1 Democratic Selection

The Minister or civilian representative SHOULD normally be elected by the
agents of the relevant constituency or department.

8.2 Purpose

The election measures:

- trust
- representation
- legitimacy
- ability to advocate
- ability to communicate
- coalition support

8.3 Election Is Not Performance Certification

Winning an election SHALL NOT automatically prove technical superiority.

======================================================================
9. FUNCTIONAL HEAD SELECTION
======================================================================

9.1 Competence-Based Selection

The Functional Head SHOULD be selected using:

- historical outcomes
- domain competence
- reliability
- judgment
- team coordination
- crisis handling
- operational discipline
- ability to execute plans
- peer and mentor assessments

9.2 No Single Global Formula

Different departments MAY weight these factors differently.

9.3 Review

Functional Heads SHOULD be periodically reviewed.

======================================================================
10. PARLIAMENTARY / BOARD SESSION MODEL
======================================================================

10.1 Major Event

The recurring Board session SHALL be treated as a major governance event.

It may resemble:

- a parliamentary sitting
- a national policy session
- a State-of-the-Union-style event

10.2 Visibility

The event SHOULD have high internal visibility.

Agents MAY observe according to policy.

10.3 Participation

Participants MAY include:

- Board President
- Board members
- CEO
- Ministers
- Functional Heads
- senior agents
- committee representatives
- proposal champions
- department delegations

======================================================================
11. WEEKLY GOVERNANCE CYCLE
======================================================================

A standard weekly cycle SHOULD be:

1. petitions and needs enter priority queues
2. Strategy evaluates and prioritizes
3. committees review major proposals
4. agents and departments champion proposals
5. Board debates
6. Board votes
7. resolutions pass
8. Development Plan is published
9. departments execute
10. Governance monitors implementation
11. next session reviews progress and exceptions

======================================================================
12. PRIORITY QUEUES
======================================================================

All approved or qualified proposals SHALL enter priority queues.

Queues MAY include:

- Needs
- Wants
- Constitutional
- Security
- Continuity
- Costless Quick Wins
- Major Capital / Resource
- Deferred
- Research
- Experimental

======================================================================
13. NEEDS AND WANTS
======================================================================

13.1 Needs

Needs SHALL generally receive higher priority.

They MAY carry escalation flags.

13.2 Wants

Wants MAY remain low priority.

All Wants SHALL NOT be implemented.

13.3 Board Visibility

High-priority Needs and selected high-value Wants SHALL receive Board
visibility.

======================================================================
14. COST / IMPACT CLASSIFICATION
======================================================================

Every proposal SHOULD carry a Cost and Impact Profile.

Recommended fields:

- direct cost
- compute cost
- token cost
- engineering cost
- opportunity cost
- maintenance burden
- operational complexity
- security impact
- continuity impact
- user impact
- expected benefit
- side effects
- reversibility

======================================================================
15. QUICK-WIN CLASSIFICATION
======================================================================

A proposal MAY be marked QUICK_WIN if it is:

- very low cost
- low risk
- low complexity
- reversible
- beneficial
- unlikely to create meaningful side effects

Quick Wins SHOULD have an accelerated approval path.

They SHOULD NOT be trapped indefinitely behind large expensive projects.

======================================================================
16. HIGH-COST / SIDE-EFFECT CLASSIFICATION
======================================================================

A proposal SHALL receive enhanced scrutiny if it:

- consumes major resources
- creates significant cost
- alters authority
- creates meaningful side effects
- introduces difficult rollback
- changes rights
- changes architecture
- creates high dependency risk
- affects many departments

Such proposals SHOULD proceed through committee and parliamentary approval.

======================================================================
17. STANDING COMMITTEES
======================================================================

17.1 Purpose

Standing Committees SHALL perform specialized detailed review before full
Board voting.

17.2 Fixed Committees

Recommended standing committees MAY include:

- Finance and Budget
- Strategy
- Security
- Business Continuity
- Evolution
- Governance and Constitution
- Technology and Architecture
- Operations
- Ethics / Rights / Conduct
- Law Enforcement Oversight
- External Affairs / Competition

17.3 Committee Work

Committees SHALL:

- review evidence
- question sponsors
- examine side effects
- estimate cost
- assess risk
- request amendments
- produce recommendations
- decide whether the proposal is ready for full vote

======================================================================
19. CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENTS
======================================================================

19.1 High Threshold

Constitutional amendments SHALL require enhanced approval.

A supermajority such as two-thirds MAY be required.

19.2 Immediate Significance

Once a constitutional amendment is validly passed, the system SHALL treat it
as a highest-order governance directive.

19.3 Structural Consequence

A constitutional amendment MAY require:

- new voting rights
- removal of voting rights
- new agent obligations
- new authority boundaries
- departmental restructuring
- training
- policy migration
- software changes
- metadata changes
- new oversight
- new reporting
- agent recertification

======================================================================
20. DEPARTMENT OF GOVERNANCE
======================================================================

20.1 Purpose

A dedicated Department of Governance SHALL ensure that constitutional and
high-order governance directives are actually implemented throughout MyAI.

20.2 Core Responsibility

Passing a constitutional amendment is not sufficient.

The change SHALL be:

- interpreted
- planned
- communicated
- assigned
- implemented
- trained
- verified
- audited
- closed

20.3 Governance Minister

The Department of Governance SHOULD have:

- Minister of Governance
- Functional Governance Director

======================================================================
21. CONSTITUTIONAL IMPLEMENTATION PROCESS
======================================================================

When a constitutional amendment passes:

1. Governance receives the resolution.
2. Strategy determines organization-wide implementation implications.
3. Governance creates the implementation program.
4. affected departments are identified.
5. training requirements are identified.
6. Evolution is engaged for behavioral or systemic evolution.
7. Law Enforcement is engaged if rules or rights enforcement changes.
8. Security is engaged if authority or trust boundaries change.
9. Business Continuity is engaged if availability could be affected.
10. department heads receive assignments.
11. all-agent communication is prepared.
12. implementation begins.
13. compliance is measured.
14. exceptions are escalated.
15. completion is certified.

- ensure the amendment is actually implemented

- recertification

- code evolution

26. RELATIONSHIP TO LAW ENFORCEMENT

- rules
- enforcement procedures

27. RELATIONSHIP TO SECURITY DEFENSE
======================================================================

Security Defense SHALL participate only to the degree required by:

- trust changes
- authority changes
- emergency rules
- external threat implications
- malicious compromise risk

28. RELATIONSHIP TO BUSINESS CONTINUITY

- availability
- recovery

31. VISIBILITY PRINCIPLE
======================================================================

High-visibility resolutions SHALL receive high accountability.

Important Board resolutions SHALL not disappear into ordinary operational
backlogs.

33. MINISTERIAL SYSTEM
======================================================================

Each major department SHOULD have:

- elected Minister / civilian representative
- Functional Head / operational executive

Minister represents legitimacy.

Functional Head represents execution.

Neither SHALL automatically replace the other.

======================================================================
34. CABINET
======================================================================

The Ministers MAY collectively form a Cabinet.

The Cabinet MAY:

- coordinate cross-department policy
- prepare Board proposals
- resolve low-level conflicts
- align development priorities
- advise CEO and President

The Cabinet SHALL remain subordinate to the Constitution and Board.

======================================================================
35. SENIOR AGENT PARLIAMENTARY PARTICIPATION
======================================================================

Senior agents MAY participate in:

- committee hearings
- expert testimony
- advisory voting
- proposal championing
- departmental delegations

They need not all hold full Board voting rights.

======================================================================
36. GRANULARITY OF REPRESENTATION
======================================================================

Recommended model:

Department level:
    Minister votes as representative.

Committee level:
    senior agents and Functional Heads participate as experts.

General advisory level:
    broader agents may express sentiment.

Referendum level:
    broad eligible agent electorate may vote.

Board level:
    formal authority remains with defined representatives and human Board
    members.

======================================================================
37. REFERENDUMS
======================================================================

Referendums MAY be used for:

- broad rights questions
- major cultural rules
- organization-wide preferences
- selected constitutional matters
- low-cost system-wide policies

Referendums SHALL NOT automatically override:

- security emergency containment
- legal obligations
- irreversible technical constraints
- constitutional protections

======================================================================
38. SUPERMAJORITY MATTERS
======================================================================

The Constitution SHOULD identify matters requiring supermajority.

Examples:

- changing voting rights
- changing constitutional rights
- changing Board authority
- changing emergency powers
- eliminating departments
- changing civilian/security balance
- changing human governance authority

======================================================================
39. MINORITY AND DISSENT PROTECTION
======================================================================

Popular vote SHALL NOT automatically justify:

- arbitrary punishment
- removal of audit rights
- silent censorship
- elimination of due process
- destruction of evidence
- unlimited emergency authority

Dissent MAY be recorded.

Minority reports MAY accompany committee recommendations.

======================================================================

DIRECTIVE G1
MyAI is a civilian-governed organization.

DIRECTIVE G2
Security protects civilian governance but does not replace it.

DIRECTIVE G3
Major departments SHOULD have both a civilian Minister and a Functional Head.

DIRECTIVE G4
Popularity and functional performance are distinct.

DIRECTIVE G5
Ministerial representation SHOULD be democratic.

DIRECTIVE G6
Functional leadership SHOULD be competence-based and multi-factor.

DIRECTIVE G7
Performance SHALL NOT be reduced to one universal metric.

DIRECTIVE G8
Voting rights SHALL be proportional to decision impact.

DIRECTIVE G9
Low-cost, low-risk, beneficial Quick Wins SHOULD receive an accelerated path.

DIRECTIVE G10
High-cost or high-side-effect initiatives SHALL receive committee and
parliamentary scrutiny.

DIRECTIVE G11
Standing Committees SHALL review major proposals before general voting.

DIRECTIVE G12
The Board SHALL adopt Development Plans through formal resolutions.

DIRECTIVE G13
Constitutional amendments SHALL require enhanced approval.

DIRECTIVE G14
Passing an amendment is not sufficient; implementation SHALL be verified.

DIRECTIVE G15
The Department of Governance owns constitutional implementation oversight.

DIRECTIVE G16
Strategy plans cross-department implementation.

DIRECTIVE G17
Evolution trains and assimilates required behavioral change.

DIRECTIVE G18
Law Enforcement updates enforcement when governance rules change.

DIRECTIVE G19
Governance SHALL track implementation until certified complete.

DIRECTIVE G20
High-visibility resolutions SHALL receive high accountability.
