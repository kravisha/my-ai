MYAI BUSINESS CONTINUITY FRAMEWORK
Architecture and Engineering Specification
Version 1.0
Date: 2026-08-23
Status: Foundational Design Specification

======================================================================
0. DOCUMENT PURPOSE
======================================================================

This document defines the Business Continuity Framework for MyAI, an adaptive,
agentic AI platform intended to provide a unified AI experience across many
models, tools, providers, data sources, devices, and business functions.

Business Continuity is a top-level organizational and architectural division.
Its purpose is to keep MyAI operating through disruption and to restore
critical functions safely and predictably after failure.

Business Continuity is broader than backup.

It includes:

- continuity of critical services
- redundancy
- graceful degradation
- failover
- disaster recovery
- backup
- restore
- recovery orchestration
- provider substitution
- continuity testing
- recovery assurance
- crisis operating modes
- clean-room restoration
- recovery after malicious compromise

Business Continuity SHALL remain separate from Security Defense.

Security Defense focuses on malicious actors, prevention, detection,
containment, and trust restoration.

Business Continuity focuses on service continuity, data survivability,
recovery objectives, alternate providers, restoration sequencing, and
operational resilience regardless of whether the root cause is malicious,
accidental, environmental, provider-related, software-related, or human.

The Department of Evolution, specified separately, will train and certify
continuity agents and help evolve continuity patterns over time.

======================================================================
1. CONSTITUTIONAL PRINCIPLES
======================================================================

1.1 Continuity by Design

Critical functions SHALL be designed with continuity requirements from the
beginning.

Continuity SHALL not be treated as a post-launch backup feature.

1.2 Adaptive Architecture

MyAI SHALL support replacement or substitution of:

- storage providers
- cloud providers
- model providers
- identity providers
- observability providers
- queue systems
- databases
- compute environments
- backup targets

without constitutional redesign.

1.3 No Single Point of Irrecoverable Failure

A single infrastructure component, provider, credential, machine, region,
agent, or administrator SHOULD NOT be able to cause permanent loss of
critical system state.

1.4 Recovery Must Be Tested

A backup that has never been restored SHALL NOT be considered a proven
recovery asset.

1.5 Recovery Must Preserve Trust

Business Continuity SHALL NOT restore systems blindly after a security event.

Security Defense SHALL confirm or help establish recovery trust.

1.6 Graceful Degradation

If full operation is impossible, MyAI SHOULD preserve the most important
functions in a reduced mode.

1.7 Provider Agnosticism

Business continuity SHALL depend on stable provider-neutral contracts.

Today's storage provider may be Dropbox.

Tomorrow's provider may be:

- local encrypted storage
- object storage
- another cloud drive
- a NAS
- an enterprise backup service
- offline storage
- multiple providers simultaneously

1.8 Recoverability Over Convenience

Operational shortcuts SHALL NOT silently weaken the ability to recover.

1.9 Versioned State

Important system state SHOULD be versioned where practical.

1.10 Separation of Primary and Recovery Failure Domains

At least one recovery copy SHOULD exist outside the same failure domain as
the primary service.

- ISO 22301 Business Continuity Management

MyAI-specific continuity controls MAY exceed conventional patterns where

======================================================================
4. STANDARD CONTINUITY AGENT LIFECYCLE
======================================================================

Each branch SHALL follow a common lifecycle:

OBSERVE
    Monitor service health, dependencies, backup status, capacity, provider
    health, replication status, recovery readiness, and external disruption.

ANALYZE
    Determine impact, severity, affected services, likely duration, and
    dependency relationships.

PRIORITIZE
    Determine which services and data must be preserved or recovered first.

DECIDE
    Select continuity actions based on RTO, RPO, cost, trust state, and risk.

ACT
    Fail over, degrade, isolate, restore, reroute, rebuild, or invoke crisis
    procedures.

VERIFY
    Confirm that service, data, security, and performance requirements are met.

RECORD
    Maintain immutable continuity and recovery evidence.

LEARN
    Feed lessons into resilience design.

CERTIFY
    Require validation before new continuity logic becomes authoritative.

======================================================================
6. RPO, RTO, AND RECOVERY OBJECTIVES
======================================================================

6.1 Recovery Point Objective

RPO defines the maximum acceptable amount of data loss measured in time.

Example:

RPO = 15 minutes

means recovery should not normally lose more than the latest 15 minutes of
committed protected data.

6.2 Recovery Time Objective

RTO defines the target time to restore a service after an outage.

6.3 Objective Classes

Recommended initial classes:

CLASS A
RPO: near-zero to minutes
RTO: minutes

CLASS B
RPO: <= 1 hour
RTO: <= 4 hours

CLASS C
RPO: <= 24 hours
RTO: <= 24 hours

CLASS D
Best effort / reconstructable

Exact values SHALL be defined per service.

6.4 Dependency-Aware Objectives

A service SHALL NOT claim an RTO faster than its critical dependencies can
support.

======================================================================
7. BACKUP ARCHITECTURE
======================================================================

7.1 Backup Is a Core Continuity Capability

Backup SHALL include all critical recoverable state, not merely source code.

Potential backup domains include:

- application configuration
- user data
- agent memory
- vector stores
- metadata
- audit records
- policy configurations
- deployment manifests
- database snapshots
- object data
- model-routing configuration
- continuity configuration
- security configuration
- infrastructure state
- selected logs
- recovery keys and manifests

Source code already protected by Git SHALL still require continuity planning
for repository compromise or loss.

7.2 3-2-1 Principle

Where practical, critical data SHOULD follow a 3-2-1 pattern:

- at least 3 copies
- on at least 2 different storage types or failure domains
- with at least 1 copy offsite or otherwise isolated from the primary failure
  domain

For critical data, an immutable or offline copy SHOULD be considered.

7.3 Backup Classes

HOT COPY
    immediately usable replicated state

WARM COPY
    near-current state requiring limited preparation

COLD COPY
    lower-cost recovery copy requiring restore

ARCHIVAL COPY
    long-term retention copy

IMMUTABLE COPY
    protected against normal modification or deletion

OFFLINE COPY
    disconnected from production access paths

7.4 Backup Metadata

Every backup set SHALL record:

- backup_id
- creation time
- source system
- source version
- schema version
- tenant scope
- data classification
- encryption method
- integrity hash
- storage provider
- retention policy
- expiration
- restore compatibility
- parent backup if incremental
- software version compatibility
- trust state

======================================================================
9. BACKUP CREDENTIALS AND AUTHENTICATION
======================================================================

9.1 Secret Handling

Backup provider credentials SHALL be handled exactly like other high-value
API credentials.

They SHALL NOT be stored in Git.

9.2 Development

Local development MAY use local secret configuration if excluded from source
control and protected by filesystem permissions.

9.3 Production

Production SHOULD use a secret manager or equivalent secure platform service.

9.4 Least Privilege

Backup credentials SHOULD be scoped only to required backup locations and
operations.

9.5 Separate Restore Authority

Where practical, restore authority SHOULD be separated from ordinary backup
write authority.

9.6 Emergency Recovery Credentials

Emergency recovery credentials SHALL be:

- strongly protected
- rarely used
- audited
- tested under controlled procedures
- rotated when exposure is suspected

======================================================================
10. ENCRYPTION AND DATA PROTECTION
======================================================================

10.1 Encrypt Before Third-Party Storage

Sensitive backup data SHOULD be encrypted before upload where practical.

10.2 Key Separation

The storage provider SHOULD not automatically hold all decryption authority.

10.3 Key Recovery

Business Continuity SHALL define how encryption keys themselves are
recoverable.

A perfectly encrypted backup with permanently lost keys is not a usable
backup.

10.4 Key Escrow

Critical recovery keys MAY use secure, split, multi-party, or escrowed
recovery mechanisms according to risk.

10.5 Integrity

Every backup SHOULD have cryptographic integrity verification.

======================================================================
11. PERSONAL HARDWARE BACKUP
======================================================================

11.1 Purpose

MyAI MAY maintain one or more personal hardware recovery copies under user
control.

This is a valid resilience layer if implemented securely.

11.2 Personal Hardware Is Not Automatically Trusted

The device SHALL be treated as another storage system with explicit security
requirements.

11.3 Minimum Requirements

Recommended controls:

- full-disk encryption
- strong local authentication
- minimal exposed services
- no public administrative interface
- firewall
- operating system updates
- hardware health monitoring
- backup versioning
- integrity verification
- malware protection appropriate to platform
- optional removable or offline copy

11.4 Pull vs Push

Where practical, a protected recovery device SHOULD pull encrypted backup
packages from a controlled endpoint rather than expose inbound public
services.

11.5 Offline Recovery Copy

For critical data, periodic offline storage SHOULD be considered to reduce
ransomware and credential-compromise risk.

12. DATA CONTINUITY
======================================================================

12.1 Database Protection

Critical databases SHOULD support one or more of:

- synchronous replication
- asynchronous replication
- point-in-time recovery
- snapshots
- transaction log archiving
- cross-region copy
- cross-account copy

12.2 Object Data

Object stores SHOULD use:

- versioning
- deletion protection
- lifecycle policies
- replication where justified

12.3 Agent Memory

Agent memory SHALL be treated as first-class recoverable state when it
contains persistent value.

Memory backups SHALL preserve:

- tenant scope
- provenance
- sensitivity
- memory version
- embedding or index compatibility metadata
- deletion/tombstone state where required

12.4 Derived Data

Derived data MAY be reconstructed instead of fully backed up if:

- source data is protected
- reconstruction process is documented
- recovery time remains acceptable

13.1 Stateless Preference

Application services SHOULD be as stateless as practical.

Persistent state SHOULD live in protected data services.

13.2 Restartability

Services SHALL be able to restart cleanly.

13.3 Idempotency

Critical workflows SHOULD be idempotent or support duplicate detection.

13.4 Queue Recovery

Message queues SHALL define:

User sessions SHOULD degrade safely if session stores fail.

17. GRACEFUL DEGRADATION
======================================================================

17.1 Principle

Degraded service is often better than complete failure.

17.2 Degraded Modes

Possible continuity modes:

NORMAL
DEGRADED
READ_ONLY
LIMITED_AI
LOCAL_ONLY
QUEUE_ONLY
RECOVERY
EMERGENCY

17.3 Examples

If vector search fails:
    use recent memory or limited indexed context.

If advanced model provider fails:
    use approved fallback model.

If file processing fails:
    keep chat available.

If a region fails:
    route to alternate region.

If storage provider fails:
    continue primary operation and queue backup transfer if risk permits.

If identity is partially degraded:
    preserve low-risk existing sessions but block privileged changes if policy

18.2 Disaster Categories

- trigger

19. RECOVERY ORCHESTRATION
======================================================================

19.1 Recovery Order

Recovery SHALL be dependency-aware.

Typical order:

1. trust and identity foundation
2. security control plane
3. continuity control plane
4. critical secrets and keys
5. core data stores
6. queue and messaging
7. core application services
8. agent runtime
9. model routing
10. memory services
11. integrations
12. noncritical services

19.2 Recovery Graph

Business Continuity SHOULD maintain a machine-readable dependency graph.

19.3 Parallel Recovery

Independent services MAY recover in parallel.

19.4 Recovery Gates

Each stage SHALL verify:

- integrity
- schema compatibility
- identity
- security posture
- connectivity
- functional health

before dependent systems proceed.

======================================================================
20. CLEAN-ROOM RECOVERY
======================================================================

20.1 Purpose

After serious compromise, recovering onto the same potentially compromised
environment may reintroduce the attacker.

20.2 Clean Room

MyAI SHOULD support rebuilding critical systems into a trusted clean
environment.

20.3 Clean-Room Inputs

Only verified artifacts SHALL enter the clean environment:

- signed or verified builds
- validated infrastructure definitions
- verified backups
- approved secrets
- trusted base images
- trusted dependencies

20.4 Security Approval

Security Defense SHALL participate in determining when recovered systems may
return to production after malicious compromise.

======================================================================
21. CRISIS OPERATIONS
======================================================================

21.1 Continuity Operating Mode

Business Continuity MAY activate crisis operating mode when normal operation
is no longer sustainable.

21.2 Crisis Priorities

Priority order SHOULD be:

1. preserve people and legal obligations
2. preserve security and trust
3. preserve critical data
4. preserve core user functions
5. preserve recoverability
6. restore noncritical features later

21.3 Feature Shedding

During crisis, the system MAY disable:

- nonessential analytics
- expensive batch work
- experimental agents
- noncritical integrations
- low-priority model calls
- cosmetic features

21.4 Capacity Reservation

Critical services MAY reserve compute, queue capacity, storage, or model
budgets for crisis operation.

======================================================================
29. BUSINESS CONTINUITY AND SECURITY DEFENSE
======================================================================

The two divisions are peers.

Business Continuity SHALL not override Security Defense simply to restore
service quickly.

Security Defense SHALL not unnecessarily block recovery once trust conditions
are met.

Joint scenarios include:

- ransomware
- destructive insider activity
- malicious data deletion
- compromised backup credentials
- DDoS plus regional failover
- poisoned deployment pipeline
- identity compromise
- malicious agent propagation
- data exfiltration with service shutdown
- control-plane compromise

======================================================================
30. SECURITY-GATED RECOVERY
======================================================================

After a malicious event, recovery SHALL include trust gates.

Before production restoration:

- compromised credentials are revoked
- known persistence mechanisms are removed
- vulnerable components are patched or mitigated
- restored backups are verified
- agent identities are reissued where needed
- trust roots are validated
- suspicious integrations are disabled
- audit systems are functioning

- narrowly scoped
- logged
- time-limited
- reviewed after use

41.3 Storage

Break-glass recovery material SHOULD exist outside the normal dependency path
that it is intended to recover.

49. FAILURE SCENARIOS
======================================================================

49.1 Backup Provider Failure

- continue local or secondary backup if available
- queue transfers
- alert continuity control plane
- switch provider according to policy

49.2 Personal Hardware Failure

- mark copy unavailable
- verify alternate copies
- replace hardware
- rebuild from trusted source
- reestablish offline or local resilience layer

49.3 Lost Backup Credential

- rotate or reauthorize
- verify access
- verify no retention gap

49.4 Corrupt Backup

- quarantine backup
- verify prior versions
- investigate source corruption
- restore only from validated copy

49.5 Lost Encryption Key

- invoke key recovery
- validate reconstructed key
- document event
- rotate if required

49.6 Region Failure

- activate alternate region or rebuild according to service tier
- verify data consistency
- reroute traffic
- validate critical functionality

49.7 Model Provider Failure

- route to approved fallback
- reduce unsupported features
- preserve task queues where appropriate

49.8 Control Plane Failure

- invoke protected fallback continuity logic
- restrict major changes
- preserve core recovery functions

50. FOUNDATIONAL CONTINUITY DIRECTIVES
======================================================================

DIRECTIVE 1
Business Continuity is a peer of Security Defense.

DIRECTIVE 2
Backup is one component of Business Continuity, not the whole discipline.

DIRECTIVE 3
Every critical service must have explicit recovery objectives.

DIRECTIVE 4
Every critical backup must be restorable and periodically tested.

DIRECTIVE 5
Critical recovery data must not exist only in the same failure domain as the
primary system.

DIRECTIVE 6
Storage providers must be replaceable through provider-neutral interfaces.

DIRECTIVE 7
Provider credentials must never be embedded in Git.

DIRECTIVE 8
Sensitive backups should be encrypted before third-party storage where
practical.

DIRECTIVE 9
Personal hardware may serve as a recovery layer, but must be treated as a
security-sensitive system.

DIRECTIVE 10
After malicious compromise, recovery must be security-gated.

DIRECTIVE 11
Critical systems should degrade gracefully rather than fail unnecessarily.

DIRECTIVE 12
No continuity agent may silently weaken backup retention, encryption, or
recovery policy.

DIRECTIVE 13
The Department of Evolution may improve continuity agents only through
controlled validation and certification.

51. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA FOR BUSINESS CONTINUITY V1
======================================================================

The initial Business Continuity architecture may be considered implemented
when:

- every Tier 0/1 service has RPO and RTO
- critical persistent state is backed up automatically
- backup adapters are provider-neutral
- Dropbox-style storage is only one adapter, not a dependency
- backup secrets are not in Git
- critical backups are encrypted
- at least one independent recovery copy exists
- local/personal hardware recovery is supported securely if enabled
- restore is tested
- recovery ordering is documented
- model provider fallback exists
- degraded operating modes exist
- backup integrity is continuously checked
- security-gated recovery exists
- critical services have runbooks
- continuity agents have explicit authority contracts
- the architecture can replace major providers without constitutional redesign
