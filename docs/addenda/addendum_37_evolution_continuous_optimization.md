MYAI DEPARTMENT OF EVOLUTION
CONTINUOUS OPTIMIZATION ADDENDUM
Version 1.0
Date: 2026-08-25
Status: Proposed Architecture Amendment

======================================================================
0. PURPOSE
======================================================================

This addendum extends the existing MyAI Department of Evolution specification
to establish Continuous Optimization as a permanent organizational principle
and operating capability.

Continuous Optimization is part of Continuous Evolution, Continuous Growth,
and Continuous Learning.

Optimization is enterprise-level optimization of the entire MyAI organization,
including model selection, agent-to-model fit, token and inference cost,
latency, routing, staffing by agent type, duplication of work, collaboration
patterns, workflow design, simulation efficiency, training efficiency,
resource use, provider dependence, reliability, organizational throughput,
and quality-versus-cost tradeoffs.

The Department of Optimization SHALL operate within the Department of Evolution.

======================================================================
1. GOVERNANCE BOUNDARY
======================================================================

Strategy decides what organizational outcomes, weaknesses, opportunities,
needs, and priorities deserve action.

Evolution converts approved mandates into controlled systemic change.

Optimization is a specialized capability inside Evolution. It determines how
an approved capability or organizational function can be delivered more
efficiently, economically, reliably, and effectively.

Optimization SHALL NOT independently redefine organizational strategy.

Optimization MAY identify opportunities or weaknesses and submit them to
Strategy for consideration.

======================================================================
2. CONTINUOUS OPTIMIZATION PRINCIPLE
======================================================================

MyAI SHALL continuously seek better combinations of intelligence, cost, speed,
reliability, specialization, collaboration, resilience, resource consumption,
and model/provider diversity.

Optimization is continuous, not a one-time engineering phase.

Cheaper is not automatically better.
Faster is not automatically better.
More intelligent is not automatically better.

The objective is the best organizational outcome per unit of constrained
resource.

======================================================================
3. DEPARTMENT OF OPTIMIZATION
======================================================================

The Department of Optimization is a sub-department of the Department of
Evolution.

Responsibilities:

1. Measure organizational resource use.
2. Benchmark alternative models and workflows.
3. Identify waste, duplication, latency, and unnecessary intelligence spend.
4. Recommend lower-cost models for tasks that do not require premium reasoning.
5. Recommend stronger models where quality failures justify additional cost.
6. Evaluate agent-to-LLM fit.
7. Evaluate routing policies.
8. Evaluate token budgets and context use.
9. Evaluate whether a task should be performed by deterministic software, a
   small/cheap model, a stronger model, multiple models, or multiple
   collaborating agents.
10. Submit proposed systemic changes through the established Evolution process.
11. Measure real-world outcomes after rollout.
12. Recommend rollback or further optimization when outcomes differ from
    prediction.

======================================================================
4. OPTIMIZATION AGENT
======================================================================

A dedicated Optimization Agent or equivalent service SHALL exist.

The Optimization Agent maintains cost measurements, model performance
measurements, latency measurements, task success measurements, collaboration
measurements, reliability measurements, model-provider benchmarks,
agent/model mapping recommendations, optimization experiments, predicted
impact, observed impact, and rollback recommendations.

======================================================================
5. OPTIMIZATION AND MULTI-LLM OPERATION
======================================================================

Initial model/task mappings may begin as human-defined default assumptions.

Examples:

- strong coding model -> development-heavy agents
- strong language/reasoning model -> language/reasoning-heavy agents
- inexpensive models -> routine, repetitive, low-risk work

These mappings SHALL NOT become permanent dogma.

They must be tested, benchmarked, and revised using observed results.

The organization must remain capable of replacing a provider or model without
redesigning every agent.

======================================================================
6. OPTIMIZATION LOOP
======================================================================

Observe
-> Measure
-> Identify inefficiency or opportunity
-> Form hypothesis
-> Model predicted effect
-> Request simulation where needed
-> Evaluate simulation results
-> Submit systemic change through approved governance
-> Train affected agents if behavior changes
-> Deploy in controlled stages
-> Observe real-world lagged effects
-> Compare predicted versus actual outcomes
-> Retain, refine, or rollback

======================================================================
7. PREDICTED VERSUS OBSERVED PERFORMANCE
======================================================================

Optimization changes may temporarily make real-world performance worse before
benefits become visible.

Therefore every meaningful change SHALL define:

- expected immediate effect
- expected short-term effect
- expected long-term effect
- expected adaptation lag
- acceptable temporary degradation
- failure thresholds
- rollback threshold
- observation window

Operations SHALL monitor whether real-world behavior follows the predicted
trajectory.

If observed outcomes materially diverge from prediction, the organization
opens a review and analyzes whether the cause is bad assumptions, poor
training, weak simulation, incomplete rollout, model mismatch, unforeseen
interaction, or operational noise.

======================================================================
8. COLLABORATION AS A NON-NEGOTIABLE OPTIMIZATION CONSTRAINT
======================================================================

Optimization SHALL NOT improve a local metric by damaging collaboration.

An agent that completes more individual work while weakening the organization
is not considered optimized.

Collaboration is the highest-value behavioral competency in MyAI.

Optimization metrics SHALL include information sharing, timely requests for
help, usefulness of responses to other agents, handoff quality, cross-agent
evidence sharing, constructive challenge, willingness to yield when another
agent has stronger evidence, contribution to other agents' success, and
avoidance of silo behavior.

======================================================================
9. LEADERSHIP AND PROMOTION RULE
======================================================================

Strong individual performance is not sufficient for leadership.

An agent that performs exceptionally in its own tasks but fails to collaborate
SHALL NOT be promoted to leadership.

Demonstrated positive collaboration is a hard promotion gate.

Leadership evaluation SHALL weight collaboration more heavily than any single
individual production metric.

======================================================================
10. RELATIONSHIP TO EDUCATION
======================================================================

When Optimization identifies a behavioral or capability gap requiring training,
it SHALL submit the requirement to the Department of Education through the
authorized organizational process.

Education determines the curriculum and training program.

======================================================================
11. RELATIONSHIP TO SIMULATION
======================================================================

Optimization may request simulation experiments to test alternative model
choices, routing changes, staffing changes, collaboration patterns, workflow
redesign, token limits, degraded provider conditions, latency/cost tradeoffs,
scaling behavior, and organizational side effects.

Simulation SHALL remain neutral.

Optimization defines the question.
Simulation produces controlled evidence.
Governance determines whether systemic change is approved.

======================================================================
12. CONSTITUTIONAL DIRECTIVES
======================================================================

O1. Continuous Optimization is a core component of Continuous Evolution.
O2. Enterprise optimization SHALL consider organizational outcome, not merely
    local agent efficiency.
O3. Model selection SHALL remain replaceable, measurable, and revisable.
O4. Low-cost models SHOULD perform low-criticality work when quality remains
    sufficient.
O5. High-capability models SHOULD be reserved for work where their additional
    capability creates meaningful organizational value.
O6. Optimization SHALL preserve collaboration as a primary constraint.
O7. Predicted and observed outcomes SHALL be compared after material changes.
O8. Systemic optimization changes SHALL remain governed, testable, staged, and
    reversible where practical.
O9. No agent may qualify for leadership without demonstrated collaboration.

======================================================================
END
