PROJECT JARVIS — SIMULATION, MARKET DATA & CORE ENGINE ARCHITECTURE
Checkpoint: August 18, 2026

1. GOVERNING PRINCIPLES
- Foundation First: stabilize shared contracts before implementation; extend rather than duplicate.
- Reuse Before Reinvent: adopt or adapt public/open components wherever practical; build only what differentiates Jarvis.
- Vendor Independence: all external providers connect through adapters into Jarvis-owned canonical formats.
- Synthetic, historical, and live data must have explicit provenance and must never be silently mixed.
- Simulation should generate coherent market states, not disconnected random prices.
- Every simulated exercise must have measurable ground truth so agent detection and explanation can be evaluated.

2. CORE ENGINES
A. Simulation Engine
Purpose: continuously generate synthetic market worlds and targeted training exercises.
Initial synthetic generation will use Monte Carlo methods.
It must support scenario composition, opportunity injection, ground truth, evaluation, replay/reproducibility, and continuous agent training.

B. Historical Market Data Engine
Ingests and replays real historical data. Historical training does not require Monte Carlo.

C. Live Market Data Engine
Ingests live production market data through provider-independent adapters.

D. Reference Data Engine
Authoritative source for security/entity reference data. It ingests, normalizes, versions, reconciles, and serves reference data through a common API.
Examples: securities, issuers, symbols, identifiers, exchanges, sectors, calendars, option contract specifications, and corporate actions.
Use real/public reference data as early as practical rather than simulating static facts.

E. Risk Engine
First-class engine for continuous risk assessment across portfolios, strategies, agents, and market states.
No opportunity/recommendation should ultimately exist without an attached risk assessment.
Monte Carlo may be reused by Risk, but Risk has a distinct objective from training simulation.

3. SHARED CANONICAL DATA CONTRACT
Simulation, historical replay, and live market data should emit the same canonical internal event/data format.
Consumers should not need to know whether the source is synthetic, historical, or live, except through explicit provenance metadata.
Reference entities should use Jarvis-owned internal IDs mapped to external identifiers.

4. DATA STORES
- Reference Data Store: authoritative relatively static entity/security facts.
- Data Store: raw observations, events, market data, news, and simulation data.
- Knowledge Store: validated lessons, patterns, relationships, and explanatory knowledge derived from evidence.
- Strategy Store: prescriptive/executable playbooks based on knowledge.
- Model Store: trained model artifacts and versions.
Conceptual flow: Data -> Knowledge -> Strategy, with models linked where appropriate.
Knowledge is descriptive (“what we have learned”); strategy is prescriptive (“what we do”).

5. PHASE-1 FINANCIAL FOCUS
Primary trading/intelligence asset classes:
- Equities
- ETFs
- Options
Other asset classes remain extensible but may have lower support maturity initially.
Bonds and credit are initially context intelligence, not primary trade-generation targets.

6. SAMPLING INTERVAL MODEL
Do not force all market information into one universal tick.
Maintain an extensible enumeration of real-world time horizons, including:
1 second, 1 minute, 1 hour, 1 day, 1 week, 2 weeks, 4 weeks, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 2 years, 3 years, 5 years, 7 years, 10 years, 15 years, and 30 years.
“Sampling Interval” is the preferred term for how frequently a market-data element is sampled/generated.
Each market-data type should have rules connecting it to appropriate sampling intervals.

Equities: one-minute sampling is the initial working default.
Options: adaptive sampling based on liquidity. High-volume/liquid contracts can use one-minute data; lower-volume contracts can move to five-minute or longer intervals.
The policy should consider volume, open interest, and/or trade frequency.

7. BOND AND CREDIT INTELLIGENCE
Jarvis should monitor bond and credit markets even before generating bond trades.
Acquire/ingest raw underlying observations where possible and calculate analytics internally.
Relevant inputs/outputs include:
- Government bond prices/yields
- Yield curves
- Corporate bond prices/yields
- Credit spreads and spread curves
- Single-name credit curves
Prefer raw vendor/public data plus Jarvis-owned analytics rather than dependence on vendor-computed intelligence.
Bloomberg may be added later, but architecture must not depend on Bloomberg.

8. SIMULATION SCENARIO TAXONOMY
Build a comprehensive scenario library covering recognizable market environments and shocks, including:
- Bull, bear, sideways, and transition regimes
- Low/high/changing volatility
- Liquidity expansion and liquidity freezes
- Market crashes and recoveries
- Credit crises
- Real-estate stress
- Sector/industry collapses and booms
- Wars and geopolitical shocks
- Political and social tensions
- Epidemics/pandemics
- Climate-related shocks
- Fraud/accounting/corporate scandals
- Rate and macroeconomic shocks
- Event-driven company and market situations
The taxonomy must be extensible and should draw lessons from decades of observed market history without simply replaying history.

9. SIMULATION CONSTRUCTION
Do not regenerate an entirely separate market universe for every exercise.
Use layered composition:
1. Base Market Generator — coherent baseline prices, volume, volatility surfaces, rates/credit context, etc.
2. Scenario Modifiers — crash, boom, rate shock, liquidity freeze, geopolitical event, and other regime transformations.
3. Opportunity Injector — controlled, intentional skews/mispricings/signals.
4. Ground Truth / Answer Key — what opportunities exist, their strength, timing, and intended interpretation.
This lets the same opportunity be tested across multiple regimes and avoids duplicated generation logic.

10. OPPORTUNITY-DRIVEN TRAINING
The Simulation Engine exists to train agents to identify opportunities, not merely to produce random synthetic prices.
Generate realistic but controlled skews, including volatility skew and other coherent cross-market relationships.
Agents must detect the opportunity and articulate/explain it.
Exercises should include subtle opportunities, conflicting signals, false positives, and varying signal strength so agents learn discernment rather than rote pattern matching.

11. THREE TRAINING STAGES
Stage 1 — Synthetic Simulation:
Monte Carlo-driven synthetic worlds and scenario exercises; continuous training.

Stage 2 — Historical Replay:
Real historical data used for training/evaluation; Monte Carlo is not required.

Stage 3 — Live:
Real-time market conditions and live data; Monte Carlo is not required for the data source.

All three stages should share compatible interfaces and a unified evaluation philosophy.

12. EXISTING JARVIS SIMULATION PRINCIPLES TO PRESERVE
- Continuous baseline simulation and analyst-requested targeted simulation are both first-class responsibilities.
- “Analyst asks. Simulation Engine executes. Analyst evaluates.”
- Later simulated periods inherit state rather than resetting by default.
- Simulation supports organizational correctness, workflows, permissions, memory, recovery, training, evaluation, and certification.
- Strategy backtesting remains distinct from the synthetic organizational simulation layer.
- Simulation quality control, scenario analysis, request contracts, and measurable certification gates remain required concepts.

13. NEXT DESIGN DOCUMENTS / WORKSTREAMS
Maintain separate specifications for:
- Simulation Engine
- Market Data Engine, with Historical and Live sub-engines
- Reference Data Engine
- Risk Engine
Shared specifications should cover canonical event/data contracts, provenance, identifiers, stores, and common reusable services.
